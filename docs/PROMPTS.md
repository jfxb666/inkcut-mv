# Prompts

Two kinds of prompt, generated by two different services, with different failure modes.

## Performance footage (`work/prompts/<id>.txt`)

Four blocks, in this order. The order is not cosmetic: the service reads the whole prompt as
one instruction, and a style clause placed after the action is weighted less than one placed
before it.

```
<Picture 1> is the character and setting reference: <what the reference shows, in one sentence>.

Action: <what the body does, beat by beat. Physical verbs. Where the weight is.>

Camera: <one move, with a qualifier. "slow steady dolly-in, very slight handheld float".>

Style: <the locked style clause, identical across every shot.>
```

### The style clause must be byte-identical in every shot

That is what holds nine independent generations together. Ours:

> Chinese ink-wash painting fused with hand-cut paper collage - hard scissor-cut silhouettes,
> flat ink washes with visible bleed edges, cream rice-paper ground with fibres. Limited
> palette: ink black, deep indigo, snow white, one warm amber point. Cinematic 16:9. No text,
> no letters, no subtitles, no watermark, no border, no signature. Continuous single take, no
> cuts.

Note the negative list. `no text` matters most: without it the model writes legible-looking
nonsense into shop signs and book covers, and it lands inside the frame.

### Action: write performance, not mood

Bad: *"he looks sad in the snow."* The model renders a still man in snow.
Good: *"he keeps his head down, hands in pockets, shoulders hunched; the wind pushes his long
scarf out sideways and it ripples and snaps; his coat hem sways with each step."*

Every shot needs at least one thing that **moves independently of the camera** - a scarf, a
hem, hair, snow, a hand. Without it the generation is a slow zoom on a static figure, and no
amount of compositing rescues that.

### Camera: one move

Two moves in one prompt produces neither. And avoid `cuts` - the service will happily edit
inside the clip, which breaks the shot's timing.

### Reference images

- One figure, full body, plain background. A turnaround sheet (several views on one canvas)
  makes the model draw several people into one shot.
- The reference must already be in the final art style. **If the reference is photographic,
  the output is photographic** no matter what the style clause says.
- Reuse the same reference for every shot containing that character. That is the entire
  consistency mechanism.

## Still plates (`work/prompts/<id>.plate.txt`)

A plate is background art: no people, no action, wide. One paragraph, art direction first,
subject second.

```
<medium + texture + edge treatment + palette + framing + negatives>. <the actual scene>.
```

Plates are the layer the clip composites over, and the fallback when a clip is missing, so
they must be compositionally usable on their own. Shoot them slightly wider than you think you
need: the composition re-frames each shot on its internal beats, and a tight plate has nothing
left to crop into.

### The photographic-drift failure

Two of nine plates came back as photorealistic renders - the model ignored "ink-wash" because
the subject (a man walking, a woman standing) pulled it toward photography. What fixed it was
naming the failure explicitly and describing the *mark-making*:

> Flat 2D illustration, hand-cut paper collage with watercolour ink wash, **NOT a photograph**.
> Every shape has a hard scissor-cut edge with a 1px cream paper margin, and the interiors are
> flat ink washes with visible bleed edges - **the face is rendered with two ink dots and one
> brush stroke, not with shading**.

That last clause is the load-bearing one: it tells the model *how* to draw a face in this
medium, instead of leaving it to infer shading, which is what drags it toward realism.

## Prompt hygiene that saves quota

- Write every prompt to a file. `scripts/gen-clips.mjs` reads them from disk and matches jobs
  to shots by content - an inline prompt cannot be matched back after a crash.
- Never let one prompt be a prefix of another. See `docs/PITFALLS.md`.
- Keep the first sentence of each prompt distinctive; it is what a human reads in the job list.
